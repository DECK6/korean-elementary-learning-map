# Korean Subject Redesign Notes

## Summary

This note records how Marble's learning-graph approach inspired the design discussion while the Korean seed and subject model were independently built around Korean schooling, public curriculum documents, and local cultural assumptions. The result is not a translation or an official derivative of Marble.

## 국어

국어 starts from Korean language development: Hangul decoding, syllable-block reading, Korean grammar, Korean discourse participation, Korean literature, and media literacy. It should not inherit native English literacy milestones such as Common Core ELA sentence/phonics assumptions without adaptation.

## 영어 as EFL

Elementary English in Korea is a foreign-language curriculum. The graph should prioritize listening exposure, familiar classroom phrases, sound recognition, formulaic speaking, alphabet/phonics as L2 print, and gradual reading/writing. This differs from native English Language Arts, where learners already speak the language and literacy instruction builds on native oral language.

## 사회 / 한국사 / 지리 / 시민성

Social studies must be Korea-centered. Early anchors should start from the learner's school, local community, Korean regions, maps, local change, democratic participation, and Korean historical/civic context. US state history, UK national history, or generic Western civic examples are not appropriate defaults.

## 수학

Mathematics can share some universal concepts with the original taxonomy, but sequencing, domain names, examples, and grade-band expectations should follow the Korean 2022 curriculum.

## 과학

Science should preserve inquiry practices and Korean elementary domain framing. The staged seed uses representative inquiry, matter, life, motion/energy, and Earth/space anchors while deferring a full prerequisite graph.

## 도덕

Moral Education is first-class in the Korean curriculum. It should be modeled around self, relationships, community, coexistence, peace, and ecology rather than folded into generic social-emotional learning.

## 실과 / 정보

Practical Arts links everyday life, family, resources, technology, making, and information/digital problem solving. It is not merely vocational skills; it is a Korean elementary practical-literacy strand.

## 통합교과

The integrated subjects for grades 1–2 are structurally important. They bridge first-school-life routines, inquiry into surrounding life, and expressive/cooperative activity through 바른 생활, 슬기로운 생활, and 즐거운 생활.

## Full-Depth Integration Status

The full-depth integration now merges all subject workstreams into repository-level KR files. Current coverage is 11 curricula, 620 achievement-standard anchors, 1956 micro-topics, 416 official plus 1875 candidate dependencies, and 152 clusters. Every curriculum is bound to an exact reviewed code-inventory digest and a direct official PDF source. See `docs/kr-full-depth-integration-report.md` for exact subject counts and remaining gaps.

## Remaining Review

The 620 current code anchors are `official-source-checked` against reviewed attachment inventories and item-level locators. The repository-authored summaries, topics, evidence criteria, prompts, and prerequisite edges still require subject-expert and classroom review. The cited documents are state-published public materials openly available from their original sources, so provenance records a cleared rights status alongside verified code identity.

## 미술 / 음악 / 체육

Art, music, and physical education are first-class curricula for grades 3–6, rebuilt from the exact elementary inventories in Annexes 13, 12, and 11. The earlier standalone grade 1–2 synthetic code families were removed; grades 1–2 arts and movement foundations stay under integrated-subject learning rather than invented `[2미]`, `[2음]`, or `[2체]` anchors.
